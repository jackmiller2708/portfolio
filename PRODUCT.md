# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary audience is technical leads, engineering managers, founders, and senior frontend engineers responsible for Angular applications that have become difficult to reason about. They arrive with systems where state ownership, RxJS flows, data access, boundaries, caching, and error handling have started to blur.

The site also speaks to technical reviewers who want evidence that the work is more than presentation: they should be able to inspect the architecture, copy, interactions, and page behavior and see the same discipline the portfolio claims to sell.

## Product Purpose

This portfolio exists to turn Nguyen Ngoc Huy / Jack Miller's frontend systems expertise into a clear consulting surface. It positions him for selective Angular audits, refactor planning, stabilization work, and architecture advisory, with a focus on making complex systems explicit, safer to change, and easier to evolve.

Success means a good-fit visitor quickly understands the specialization, sees enough technical proof to trust the claim, and has a clear path toward a diagnostic or contact conversation. Poor-fit work should filter itself out before the contact step.

## Positioning

Complex Angular frontend systems made explicit: state, data flow, errors, and boundaries turned from hidden risk into sequenced decisions.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary and secondary CTA: `Start a diagnostic` / `See past work`.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: Complex systems, made explicit.
- Belief ladder: this person understands the failure modes inside large Angular systems; the work is specific and technical, not generic frontend polish; the portfolio itself demonstrates explicit boundaries, accessible motion, and disciplined tradeoffs; a scoped diagnostic is a low-risk first step.
- Proof on hand: case studies, sample audit findings, technical lab posts, the interactive system map, and documented quality gates in the repository.

## Brand Personality

Calm, technical, accountable. The voice should feel like a trusted senior engineer thinking out loud with a peer: precise without becoming sterile, confident without performing confidence, and personal enough that trust attaches to a named human rather than a faceless studio.

The visual and interaction philosophy is deliberately layered. At a glance, the interface may look simple and restrained, but the details should reveal technical depth: assertive transitions, careful state changes, smooth reduced-motion alternatives, and interactions that feel engineered rather than decorated. The point is not visual noise; it is the quiet proof that simple surfaces can hide sophisticated implementation.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic agency/studio branding, broad "frontend developer" positioning, and anything that makes the site feel like a shallow UI because there is no deeper system underneath. Simplicity itself is not the enemy; shallow simplicity is. The site should not rely on static brochure polish, cheap ticket-work signals, vague methodology copy, or UI-only prettiness that cannot survive technical scrutiny.

Avoid pretending to be a product company or an abstract brand called "Frontend Systems." The named engineer, the Angular specialization, the Vietnam-based context, and the contractor boundaries should remain visible.

## Design Principles

Show the system beneath the surface. Every major page should make hidden complexity legible through structure, motion, evidence, or interaction, not through claims alone.

Let restraint carry tension. The page can look quiet at first, but important interactions should have enough precision and confidence to reveal the engineering philosophy behind them.

Make tradeoffs explicit. Copy, diagrams, examples, and case studies should connect symptoms to causes, risks, and next steps.

Filter as much as persuade. Good-fit visitors should feel invited; poor-fit work should recognize itself as out of scope.

Attach trust to a real engineer. The brand should keep Nguyen Ngoc Huy / Jack present as the accountable specialist behind the work.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and interaction quality. Keep focus states visible, preserve keyboard access, respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, and ensure animation enhances comprehension without becoming a prerequisite for understanding the page.
